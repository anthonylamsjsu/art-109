In a world that can often seem hopeless, people still make wishes during the moments when they feel most charged with hope. We wish upon shooting stars seen from the middle of traffic, blow out birthday candles, and quietly hope for things we may never say aloud. I wanted to transform this familiar act into 🅦🅞🅡🅛🅓 🅦🅘🅓🅔 🅦🅘🅢🅗, an interactive archive of anonymous wishes. The project was inspired by Yoko Ono’s Wish Tree. I was drawn to the simplicity of the work and the way it turns private wishes into a communal and hopeful experience. The archive combines personal and imagined wishes with wishes collected from friends and family. Their names and personal histories are not included, allowing the wishes to exist as shared human thoughts instead of belonging to identifiable individuals. Each statement is partially covered by a redacted bar. Visitors must click the redaction to reveal the complete wish. This creates interaction and mystery while representing how wishes are often private, hidden, or difficult to admit. I paired every wish with a black-and-white photograph that offers a glimpse into its meaning. Rather than illustrating each wish literally, the photographs frame the statements in a broad and surreal way. The music adds a dreamlike atmosphere with a slight sense of eeriness. I want visitors to experience recognition and connection as they uncover the archive. Even when a wish comes from someone else, it may reflect a fear, hope, or desire that the visitor understands. The restriction of using only HTML led me toward a simple structure and limited visual palette. The default appearance of HTML also gives the project the feeling of an early website from the 1990s.
